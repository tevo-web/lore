One ordinary day.

Here is how Lore fits into it.

7:40 AM

You wonder something. You just ask.

Every new tab starts with where you left off: your last chat, the pages you read, the sites you use. Or just ask: type a question, not an address. Lore answers in a line or two and shows where it came from. It never guesses a price or a flight.

9:15 AM

A long article, a short morning.

Lore sits beside the page, in one box: ask, or tell it what to do. It sums the page up, or answers "does it mention a price?" by quoting the exact line. Each page keeps its own chat, on your computer.

Sample summary from a stand-in model.

9:40 AM

Open a second page. Keep talking.

Ask about one page, switch tabs and ask again: Lore adds the new page to the same conversation and keeps the first as a source. Its answer says which page each claim came from, and a click takes you there.

Answer written by a stand-in model.

10:20 AM

Twelve tabs, one tidy-up.

Too many tabs open? They live in a list on the left, and Lore reads their titles and groups them by what they are about: a trip, a project, a topic. You see the plan first, change what you like, press Apply, and undo if you change your mind. Duplicates are found too.

Groups made by a stand-in model.

11:30 AM

The email you've been putting off.

Tell Lore what you need. It writes the draft and opens it in your own Gmail or Outlook. You read it, you press Send. That last part stays yours.

Sample draft from a stand-in model.

1:00 PM

Read on one side, write on the other.

Open two pages side by side and drag the divider where you like. Click into either one and Lore follows, so the tools and the address always belong to the page you are on.

2:00 PM

Wait, where was that page?

You remember the idea, not the address. Ask by meaning and Lore finds it, from a memory that is encrypted and lives only on your computer. It can also write a short note on what you read today.

And the things you expect.

A browser has to do the basics well, too.

  • ProfilesWork and personal apart: their own logins, tabs, memory and bookmarks.
  • Light, dark, or yoursFollows your system, or stays the way you pick.
  • Offline? Play.A small lotus game while you wait. The page reloads when the connection is back.
  • Bookmarks, history, downloadsA bookmarks bar, a history page by day, a downloads list, find in page, and your tabs back after a restart. They open over the page, not in windows of their own.
  • Updates itselfLore tells you when a new version is out, and installs it on Windows and Linux.
  • One bar for everythingAs you type, a small label says what Enter will do: open a site, ask Lore, write, or search your memory.
  • Tabs on the sideA list on the left with names you can read, groups as coloured blocks, and a slim icon-only mode on one key.
  • Compare tabs and ask a PDFPick two to four pages and get one table; figures not found on the page are flagged. PDFs open in the tab and answer with the page number.
  • Privacy at a glanceClick the shield: ads, tracking links, https-first and Memory, with per-site switches. A weekly report, encrypted DNS, and tabs that destroy themselves.
  • Passwords, kept hereSaved in your system keychain, filled only when you ask and only on the right site. Checks for breaches without sending the password.
  • The rest of a browserChoose your search engine, clear data by type, print or save as PDF, zoom per site, view source, a reading list, and a page that recovers when a tab crashes.

The honest part

Lore is young. We would rather tell you now than let you find out.

  • No phone version yet, and the Linux builds are new
  • It cannot pay a real bill or book a real ticket yet
  • No VPN, sync or extensions
  • The password manager has no import or passkeys, and addresses are not auto-filled
  • The installer is not signed, so your computer will frown at it

macOS blocks the app

It is not signed yet. Drag Lore to Applications, right-click it, choose Open, and confirm. Once is enough.

Still will not open, or it says the app is damaged? Run this in Terminal, then open Lore again:

xattr -cr /Applications/Lore.app
Windows warns "protected your PC"

Click More info, then Run anyway. Prefer not to trust a binary? Build it yourself.

Build from source

Needs Node.js 22+ and Git.

git clone https://github.com/tevo-web/lore.git
cd lore/desktop
npm ci
npm run filters   # compile the ad-block lists (needs internet)
npm start

Questions

Is it free?

Yes: open source, no account, no ads. A cloud AI key you add may cost money. A model on your computer does not.

Is it really private?

No analytics, no telemetry. Your history and memory stay on your computer, encrypted. "Private" is not "anonymous": sites still see your connection, and a cloud AI sees what you send it. Every result says where text went.

Do I need an AI model?

No. Answers, memory, ad blocking and scam warnings work without one. A model adds writing, summaries and translation.

How do I keep cloud AI costs down?

Settings > AI model > "Data sent to the model": Save sends only the key sentences of a page. Answers you already asked for are shown again for free, and every answer from a cloud model shows how many tokens it used.

Can I bring my bookmarks and history?

Yes, from Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, Cốc Cốc and Firefox on this computer, or from a bookmarks file. Passwords are not imported.

Does Lore update itself?

It checks for new versions, and on Windows and Linux installs them for you. On macOS it tells you and opens the download page until the app is signed.

Can I sign in to Google?

Sometimes. Google blocks some embedded browsers. If it says no, Lore offers to open the page in your system browser.

Android or iPhone?

Planned, not started.
